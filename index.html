Windows follow documented completeness: VEI ≥ 6 since 1500 (Brown et al. 2014), VEI ≥ 7 since 900 (ice cores and tephra make these unmissable), M ≥ 8.5 instrumental era only. Whether the pre-1900 climb in documented quakes (3 → 6 → 7 → 9 → 11 per century) is record-keeping or real is tested, not assumed: declustered rates across the century of constant detection are statistically flat (χ² 15.1, trend z 1.3 — see METHODOLOGY § 16), so the documentary record is shown below but never feeds these rates. Occurrence ≠ death toll: a VEI-7 today would be catastrophic, but its toll depends on where.

    About the model

    Data. EM-DAT, the international disaster database (CRED / UCLouvain, Brussels — emdat.be), public table including historical events, snapshot 2024-03-26; 13,571 physical natural-hazard events 1900→2024 after aggregating per-country records and excluding epidemics. Used under EM-DAT's non-commercial terms with attribution.

    Model. Event occurrences above an impact threshold are a superposition of per-hazard Poisson processes. Rates are empirical counts inside per-threshold completeness windows (larger disasters are reliably recorded further back: ≥10,000 deaths from 1900, ≥1,000 from 1930, ≥100 from 1990, ≥10 from 2000), with exact Poisson confidence intervals. Sub-annual windows apply a per-hazard monthly climatology (hurricane season is real). Damage uses EM-DAT's CPI-adjusted series.

    Full methodology, citations, and limitations: METHODOLOGY.md. The earthquake deep-dive runs on the instrumental seismic catalog (195k events, live USGS top-up) and supports any point on Earth.

    Probabilities describe long-run statistical frequency, not deterministic forecasts. Impact data: EM-DAT, CRED / UCLouvain, Brussels, Belgium — www.emdat.be (non-commercial use). Seismic data: USGS/ANSS ComCat, ISC-GEM.
